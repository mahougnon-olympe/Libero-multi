# Chapter 4: The Quest for the Fairy Lake

After this unfortunate misunderstanding, the two set out in search of the fairy lake. They first had to find the map that would let them go looking for it. Georgia, the village girl, knew an old witch. She quickly went out, grabbed Jordan and then her car, which she started briskly.

But the sorcerer who had bewitched Jordan had been informed, and he had posted police officers at the entrance to the village where the witch lived.

Arriving a few meters from the village, Georgia spotted the checkpoint. Two officers, stern-faced, were stopping every car and checking faces one by one.

"Get down!" Georgia whispered to Jordan.

The prince curled up under the dashboard while Georgia drove calmly toward the checkpoint. An officer leaned in at the window:

"Papers, miss. What are you here for?"

"I'm coming to visit my grandmother, she's unwell," Georgia lied with an innocent smile.

The officer stared at her for a long moment. It was then that Georgia felt a warmth rise in her hands: her powers. Discreetly, she fixed her gaze on the officer's notebook, which burst into flames at once. While the two men scrambled to put out the fire, she stepped on the accelerator and shot off into the village's alleyways.

The witch's house was right at the far end, an old thatched-roof hut surrounded by strange jars and plants seen nowhere else. Georgia knocked three times. The door opened on its own.

"I was expecting you, little youngest of the supers," said a gravelly voice.

The old witch was seated before a steaming cauldron. She looked Jordan over from head to toe and frowned:

"You... you bear the mark of the sorcerer Kadel. A potion of indestructibility, a bewitched heart... Only the water of the fairy lake can free you. But know one thing: the lake lets itself be found only by those with a pure heart. And your heart, young prince, is still very dark."

Jordan lowered his head. For the first time since his parents' death, he felt something that resembled shame.

The witch stood, rummaged through an old chest and drew out a yellowed scroll:

"Here is the map. The lake lies beyond the Wassa forest, past the three twin mountains. The journey takes seven days. But beware: Kadel already knows you are here. He will send his creatures after you."

She then handed Georgia a small blue vial:

"And you, take this. A single drop will make your powers ten times stronger. But use it only in the direst need, for each drop will cost you part of your strength."

Georgia carefully stowed away the map and the vial. Just as they were about to set off again, sirens rang out in the distance. The officers had picked up their trail.

"Go out the back, quick!" ordered the witch, opening a hidden trapdoor beneath her rug.

The two of them dove in without thinking. The tunnel, dark and damp, led them all the way to the edge of the Wassa forest. When they came back out into the open air, the sun was already setting behind the three twin mountains, which could be seen in the distance.

"Seven days," Jordan breathed, looking at the horizon.

"Seven days," Georgia repeated. "Then let's not waste time."

And that was how the quest for the fairy lake truly began.
