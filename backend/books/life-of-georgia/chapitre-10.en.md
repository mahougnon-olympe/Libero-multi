# Chapter 10: The Battle of the Lake

"You certainly took your time," said Kadel.

The sorcerer had not come alone. All around the lake, dozens of shadow creatures stood guard, motionless as statues of tar. The fairies had taken refuge in the center of the lake, their little lights huddled together, trembling.

"Here is the deal," Kadel went on. "The prince stays with me. My finest test subject, my masterpiece... I'm not going to let him come undone in a puddle of magic water. As for you, little one, go back home, and I'll spare your life. This is my final offer of peace."

"I have a counter-offer," Georgia replied. "Set all your creatures free, including the son of the woman from Toko, and I'll promise you a fair trial instead of what's about to happen."

Kadel burst into icy laughter. "I forgot the supers had a sense of humor."

He snapped his fingers.

The shadows all attacked at once.

Georgia rose into the air, her hands on fire, and the sky blazed. Balls of flame rained down on the creatures, which writhed and dissolved into black smoke. Jordan, the ivory dagger in his fist, guarded her back: the old chief had spoken true, the blade cut through the shadows like mist, and they did not reform.

But Kadel did not stay a spectator. From high in the air he hurled green lightning bolts that Georgia dodged by a hair. One of them grazed her and shattered a boulder behind her.

"You're tiring already, little one!" cried the sorcerer. "The old witch's drop cost you dearly, didn't it? How much strength have you got left?"

He was right. Each fireball cost her more than the last. Her arms trembled, her vision blurred. She took out the blue vial: two drops remained.

"Georgia, no!" screamed Jordan, who had seen her gesture. "Look what the first one did to you!"

"We have no choice!"

She swallowed the second drop.

The world turned white. A column of fire burst from her and rose to the clouds, sweeping away with a single breath all the shadow creatures that remained. Kadel raised a shield of green energy, but the wave of flames smashed it like glass and threw him to the ground, his cloak in tatters.

Then the light went out. And Georgia fell from the sky like a stone.

"GEORGIA!"

Jordan ran and caught her just in time before she crashed down. She was burning with fever, her breathing barely perceptible. Her hair, which had shone with gold seconds earlier, had turned ash gray.

"Stay with me," Jordan whispered. "We're almost there. Stay with me."

A faint laugh rose behind him. Kadel was getting up, bloodied, half broken, but alive. And between him and them, there was no one left.

"Look at her," breathed the sorcerer, staggering toward them. "She burned herself out for you. For you! The boy who destroyed her. That's the great irony of this story, Prince Jordan: everyone who comes near you ends up burning."

He raised his hand, where a last green bolt crackled.

"Rest easy. You'll leave together."

Jordan looked at the lake, twenty steps behind him. Then at Georgia, unconscious in his arms. Then at the ivory dagger, fallen too far to reach.

So he did the only thing left to do.

He turned around, pressed Georgia against himself to shield her with his body, and offered his back to Kadel's bolt.
