# Chapter 6: The Three Twin Mountains

At the foot of the first mountain, an old engraved stone blocked their path. Georgia stepped closer and read aloud:

"Three sisters watch over the road to the lake. The first tests the body, the second tests the mind, the third tests the heart. None may pass without giving something of themselves."

"Great," sighed Jordan. "And here I thought we were just going to climb."

The first mountain lived up to its name: the Trying One. The trail was so steep that at times they had to advance on all fours, gripping the rocks. The wind blew so hard it seemed to want to tear them from the cliff face. Halfway up, Jordan collapsed, his hands bleeding.

"I won't make it," he panted.

"So this is where your story ends?" Georgia asked without stopping. "The great Prince Jordan, defeated by a rock?"

Stung to the core, Jordan got back up. Step by step, he pressed on. And when they finally reached the pass, he understood that the mountain had taken something from him: his arrogance. One no longer believes oneself above others once one has crawled in the dust.

The second mountain, the Deceiver, was covered in a thick fog. No sooner had they entered it than the paths began to multiply, all identical. Worse still: voices rose from the mist. Georgia heard her mother's voice calling her, begging her to come home to Aboula. Jordan heard those of his lost parents.

"Jordan! My son! We are here, come to us!"

Jordan took a step toward the voice, his eyes full of tears. Georgia held him back by the arm.

"That's not them. Think! Would your parents want you to lose yourself in the fog, or to go all the way to the end?"

"But... what if it really was them?"

"Then they'll wait for you on the other side. True memories don't hide in the mist."

They closed their eyes, covered their ears, and moved forward holding on to each other, guided only by the slope beneath their feet. When the fog finally lifted, the Deceiver had taken something from them in turn: their illusions. One can no longer lie to oneself once one has learned to recognize lies.

Before the third mountain, the Just One, there was neither trail nor fog. Only a stone door, and before the door, a seated figure. It was a blind old woman, wrapped in a gray shawl.

"I guard the passage of the heart," she said. "Only one of you may lay a hand on the door. If his heart is pure, it will open. If it is not, the mountain will keep him forever. Choose."

Georgia stepped forward naturally, but Jordan went ahead of her.

"No. Let me do it."

"Are you mad? Your heart is still under Kadel's spell! The mountain will take you!"

"Perhaps," said Jordan in a strangely calm voice. "But if you're the one who fails, it's all over for both of us. Me, I'm not indispensable. You're the one with the powers, you're the one with the map, you're the one who's worth something. If someone has to stay here... it's me."

And before Georgia could stop him, Jordan laid his hand on the stone door.

A long silence. Then a rumble.

The door opened.

The blind old woman smiled: "Strange... A bewitched heart, tainted, guilty... and yet, the instant it touched the stone, it thought only of saving someone else. Purity of heart is not never having done wrong, young prince. It is being ready to give everything to make it right. Pass."

On the other side of the door, an immense valley stretched as far as the eye could see. And at the very bottom, something glimmered faintly on the horizon.

The fairy lake.

But in the sky, above the three mountains, black clouds were beginning to spiral. Kadel was coming.
