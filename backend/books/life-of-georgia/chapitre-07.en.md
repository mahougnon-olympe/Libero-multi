# Chapter 7: Kadel's Shadow

They had not gone a hundred steps into the valley when the sky turned black as ink. A green lightning bolt tore through the clouds, and a figure slowly descended from the air: a long dark cloak, a pale, angular face, eyes glowing with that green light they knew all too well.

"Kadel," Jordan breathed.

"Well, well," said the sorcerer in an icy voice. "My little test subject, and his bodyguard. You've given me quite a lot of trouble."

Georgia placed herself in front of Jordan, her hands already ablaze.

"How touching," Kadel sneered. "The youngest of the supers protecting her own tormentor. Tell me, little one, does he even know why I chose him?"

Jordan frowned. "What is he talking about?"

"Oh, he doesn't know!" exclaimed Kadel with a wicked laugh. "Then let me tell you, Prince Jordan. Twenty years ago, I was the official magician of the kingdom of Black-Fish. I served your father faithfully. But the day one of my experiments went wrong, your father banished me like a dog, I who had given everything for the crown! So I swore to destroy his bloodline. Not by killing you, no... that's too simple. By turning you into a monster, so the whole kingdom could see what the royal family carries in its blood. And I must say, you exceeded all my hopes."

Jordan turned pale. All the harm he had done, that dark pleasure he felt in making others suffer... it was not only him. It was this man's doing. But instead of relieving him, this truth made him sick. For spell or no spell, it was his own hands that had acted. It was Georgia who had paid.

"Give him back his heart," said Georgia in a voice trembling with anger.

"His heart? But I didn't steal it, little one. I merely... darkened it. And in a few minutes, the question will no longer matter."

Kadel raised his arms. The earth split open, and dozens of shadow creatures burst forth, surrounding the two travelers.

The battle was terrible. Georgia threw fireball after fireball, but for every creature destroyed, two more sprang up. Jordan, armed with a simple stick, struck with all his might, but the shadows reformed endlessly. Soon they were back to back, exhausted, hemmed in on all sides.

"Georgia," Jordan panted, "the vial! The witch said: in the direst need!"

"Each drop will cost me part of my strength..."

"We have no choice anymore!"

Georgia took out the little blue vial, opened it, and let a single drop fall onto her tongue.

The effect was immediate. Her hair blazed with a golden light, her feet left the ground, and a wave of fire exploded around her in a circle, sweeping away all the shadow creatures at once. Even Kadel was thrown backward, his cloak in flames.

The sorcerer rose slowly, and for the first time, something other than contempt could be read in his eyes: caution.

"Interesting," he hissed. "But look at yourself, little one. Look at what it costs you."

Georgia dropped to the ground, knees down. Her hands were trembling, her face had turned gray, and a trickle of blood ran from her nose. The drop had given her immense power, but the witch had not lied: something within her had burned away.

"We'll meet again at the lake," said Kadel as he rose into the air. "And next time, I won't be the one who leaves wounded."

He vanished into the black clouds, which closed behind him.

Jordan rushed to Georgia and caught her before she collapsed completely.

"Georgia! Stay with me. Georgia!"

"I'm all right..." she murmured. "I just... need to sleep a little."

She lost consciousness in his arms. And that night, for the first time in his life, Prince Jordan watched over someone other than himself.
