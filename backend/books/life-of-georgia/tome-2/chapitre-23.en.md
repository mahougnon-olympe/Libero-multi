# Chapter 23: The Silent Lake

Three days after Toko, they crossed the Wassa forest, and on the morning of the fourth day, they came out onto the fairy lake.

Naya stopped dead, and for a long moment, she forgot to breathe.

The tales of Kpassè had exaggerated nothing. The water was pure silver, so calm it seemed solid, and above its surface danced hundreds of small lights, white, blue and golden, as if all the stars of the sky had come down to drink.

But Jordan was not smiling. He walked to the shore, knelt as he had long ago, and spoke in a loud, respectful voice.

"Guardians of the lake, it is Jordan of Black-Fish. You judged me once, and you left me a chance I have never stopped repaying. Today I come back to ask for your help, for the evil you defeated has made a student."

The lights danced. None came closer.

"Guardians," Jordan insisted, "Georgia has disappeared. Georgia, your champion, the one who offered to give up her powers to save an enemy. If you know where she is, I beg you, answer."

The lights danced. The lake stayed silent.

Jordan remained kneeling a long time, and Naya watched his shoulders sink little by little. Sergeant Tovi murmured that perhaps the fairies had gone, that perhaps the tales had lied. But Naya was no longer listening. She had come up to the shore in her turn, and she was watching the small lights with her extra eye.

And what she saw squeezed her heart.

"Majesty," she said softly. "They are not refusing to answer. They cannot."

Jordan raised his head.

"Explain."

"You see lights dancing. I see lights struggling. Each of them is surrounded by green threads, very fine, dozens of threads, like gnats caught in a web. The threads pull, gently, without stopping, and at every beat, a little of their light flows away along the threads, toward the north. They are not dancing, Majesty. They are fighting. For months, perhaps years, they have been draining away drop by drop, and they no longer even have the strength to speak."

She followed with her eyes the green threads that rose from each fairy, joined above the lake into a cable of sick light, thick as a tree trunk, and streamed away north over the hills.

"Kadel marked men to give them magic and control them," she continued, her voice trembling. "His student has turned the spell inside out. He does not give. He takes. He drains everything that has magic, and he is starting with the source of all the magic in the world. The marked villages in a circle around the lake, Majesty: that is not a wall to keep people out. It is a net. And the lake is the fish."

A terrible silence fell on the shore. The soldiers looked at the small lights, and now that they had been told, they almost believed they too could see the weariness in their dance.

Jordan rose slowly. There was something on his face Naya had never seen there before: not the king's weariness, not the old prince's hardness. Something simpler and more frightening: fear.

"Fifteen years ago," he said, "the fairies defeated Kadel with a single gesture. They took back all his magic in one wingbeat, because no stolen power can withstand those who are the source. That was the world's guarantee, Naya. The dam behind all the dams. Whatever happened, the fairies were stronger."

"And he understood that," Naya finished. "So before doing anything else, he attacked the dam."

She looked at the great cable of stolen light fleeing northward, and a question rose in her, so cold she hardly dared ask it.

"Majesty. A spell like this, draining the fairy lake itself, holding hundreds of marked across the whole kingdom... no man has strength enough to feed that, does he? He needs a source. An enormous source."

Jordan looked at her, and she saw that he had just thought exactly the same thing.

On the shore of the silent lake, no one spoke the name. But everyone heard it.
