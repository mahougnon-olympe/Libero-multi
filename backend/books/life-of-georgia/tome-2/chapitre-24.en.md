# Chapter 24: Georgia's Trail

It was Naya who found the trace, the next morning, walking along the northern shore of the lake.

On a flat rock, half erased by a year of rains, there shone for her eye alone a silver handprint, exactly like the one on the door in Aboula.

"Majesty! Here! She passed this way!"

Jordan came running, and Naya explained to him what she had understood since Aboula without daring to believe it: Georgia left traces. Not ordinary traces: imprints of her own magic, placed deliberately, a hand pressed on stone or wood, leaving a little of her light in it.

"But why leave marks that almost no one in the world can see?" asked Sergeant Tovi.

"Because she was not leaving them for us," Jordan suddenly understood. "She was leaving them for the fairies. They see everything that is magic. Georgia was marking her route so that the guardians of the lake could follow her with their eyes and send help if she did not come back. She could not know that the fairies were already being caught in the net."

They followed the prints northward. There was one roughly every thousand paces, on a rock, a trunk, a roadside marker, and Naya almost ran from one to the next, like a child following white pebbles in a fairy tale. At the end of the second day, the prints left the road and plunged into a hidden hollow, and there, under an overhang of rock, they found the camp.

It had been abandoned for a long time. A circle of stones for the fire, a rolled-up mat eaten away by damp, a gourd. And wedged in a crack of the rock, sheltered from the rain, a small notebook with a leather cover.

Jordan picked it up with hands that trembled a little. He opened it, recognised the handwriting, closed his eyes for a moment, then read aloud, by the light of the fire the soldiers had just lit.

The notebook told of an entire year. Georgia had seen her first green mark on the wrist of a woodcutter, near the Wassa forest, and had told no one, as always, so as not to worry anyone, as always. She had consulted the old witch, then followed the trail of the carts, alone, from village to village, exactly as they had, but a year ahead of them and without an extra eye, on the strength of instinct and questioning.

Page after page, Naya listened to Jordan's voice reading the words of this woman she had never met, and she felt as if she were walking beside her. Georgia guessing the circle around the lake. Georgia understanding, well before them, that the spell had been inverted. Georgia writing, one evening: it is not Kadel, it is someone who did his homework, and that simple sentence sent a chill down the spine.

Then the last pages, in a faster, harder hand.

I have found the fortress. He rebuilt on Kadel's ruins, out of defiance or homage, I do not know. Marked everywhere, carts coming in day and night. Tomorrow I will go in by the eastern ravine, where the walls are

The sentence stopped in the middle, in a pen stroke that trailed toward the bottom of the page, like a hand interrupted.

Jordan stayed a long time with the notebook open on his knees, looking at that pen stroke. Around the fire, no one spoke. At last the king closed the notebook, tucked it against his chest, under his shirt, and when he raised his head, the fear had left his face. Only the decision remained.

"She is alive," he said.

"Majesty," Sergeant Tovi began cautiously, "a year has passed, and nothing proves..."

"She is alive, sergeant," Jordan cut in without raising his voice. "A man who drains the magic of the whole world does not kill the greatest source of magic the world has ever carried. He keeps her."

He turned toward the north, where the night sky, far away above the hills, glowed faintly with a sickly green.

"He has her plugged in."
