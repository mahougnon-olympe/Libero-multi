# Chapter 29: The Flaw

When Jordan returned to Black-Fish, he found the palace transformed into a beehive. The supers of Aboula had arrived.

Georgia's family in full had answered the king's call: the father despite his age, the sister with hands of steel, the brother swift as the wind, the cousins, the uncles, some twenty supers in all, and with them half of Aboula, come on foot because you do not leave your protector in a cocoon.

They gathered in the map room, and Georgia's father asked the question everyone was carrying.

"So, how do we get in there?"

Sergeant Tovi spread out the sketches Naya had drawn from memory: the fortress, the walls, the towers, and above all the defences her eye alone had seen.

"That is the whole problem," she said. "The walls can be climbed; the marked can be pushed aside without killing them. But around the great hall, there is something else. Veils. Three envelopes of magic one inside the other, like onion skins. The first puts to sleep. The second raises the alarm. The third... the third drains. Whoever crosses it with magic in them is sucked dry on the spot and feeds the cocoon. That is how Georgia was taken. She went in by force; she must have crossed the veils burning everything, and the more she burned, the more she fed him. Her power was turned against her. The door was designed for her."

A discouraged silence settled. The brother swift as the wind struck the table.

"So what? The stronger we are, the more surely we lose? What door opens with weakness?"

And it was on hearing that sentence that Naya went still.

She looked at her hands. She thought back to Kpassè, to the grey smoke, to the years of shame, to her father saying you will close that extra eye of yours, to a whole life spent being the girl who had nothing: no fire, no steel, no wind, nothing but a gaze.

"Me," she said.

The whole room turned to her.

"The veils detect magic," she explained slowly, thinking aloud. "They put to sleep what is magical, raise the alarm at what is magical, drain what is magical. But I have no magic. Not a drop. My eye casts nothing, produces nothing; it only sees. To Sètondji's defences, I am... nothing. A draught of air. I can walk through the three veils the way you walk through a ray of sunlight."

Georgia's father shook his head.

"Little one, there are marked sentinels in every corridor. They do not need magic to see you; they have eyes."

"Then they must be looking elsewhere," said Jordan, who had not taken his eyes off Naya. "All of them at the same time."

And the plan drew itself on the table, through the night, while the servants brought coffee and Georgia's father moved wooden pawns. The supers of Aboula and the king's soldiers would attack the fortress head-on, in the morning, with the greatest possible din. Not to win: to occupy. Every one of Sètondji's threads, every sentinel, every veil of alarm would be turned toward the battle. Meanwhile, a sixteen-year-old girl, without an ounce of magic on her, would enter by the eastern ravine with an ivory dagger.

"And once you are before the cocoon?" asked the sister with hands of steel. "You cut the threads?"

"No," said Naya. "The cocoon is not held by outside threads; it feeds from within, on Georgia herself. Cutting would do nothing; it would reweave itself at once. There is only one person in the world who can put out that cocoon."

"Who?" asked the father.

"Georgia."

Naya looked at the old father, and she chose her words with infinite care, because she was about to ask this family to believe a terrible thing.

"The cocoon lives on her fire. As long as she burns, as long as she fights, as long as she is Georgia, it holds. To kill it, she must do the one thing she has never done in her whole life. She must let go. Put everything out. Stop fighting."

The silence was long. Then the old father gave a laugh that was gentle and broken at once.

"Asking my daughter to stop fighting. Little one, you are leaving on the hardest mission of this war, and it is not crossing the walls."

Jordan laid his hand on Naya's shoulder.

"That is why we are sending you," he said. "We are not sending a warrior. We are sending someone who knows what it is to live without power."

The attack was set for the third morning.
