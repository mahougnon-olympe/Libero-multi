# Chapter 26: Sètondji's Offer

They were served food in a bright room, and Sètondji sat down across from them like an ordinary host. Naya noticed that the dishes were tasted in front of them by the master of the house himself, to show they were not poisoned. This man thought of everything, and he wanted it known.

"Fifteen years ago," he began, "a sorcerer proposed a bargain on the shore of a lake. Join me and I will spare you, he said, lying. I know the story by heart, Majesty; you are the one who lived it. So know that I am not going to replay it. I threaten no one. I offer, and my offer is sincere."

Naya watched his mouth. Nothing. Not a wisp of smoke.

"King Jordan. You have spent fifteen years repairing the kingdom, and you are tired, it shows from far away. How many crimes this year in Black-Fish? How many children dead of fever for want of a healer? You do your best, and your best is admirable, and people die anyway, every day, from a thousand negligences no one can watch over all at once. I can. Give me your legitimacy, govern with me, and in ten years there will be no more famine in this kingdom, no brigands, no dark sorcerers. You have seen my granaries. You know I do not dream. I calculate."

Jordan did not answer. Sètondji then turned to Naya, and his gaze changed, became more attentive, almost warm.

"And you. Naya of Kpassè. I had people ask who you were, you know. The girl the village ran from. The one they were going to marry off by force to put out her eye. Sixteen years of curse for a gift that is born once a century."

He leaned forward.

"Stay here, and I will make you the most honoured person in the kingdom. A weave-seer at my side is the guarantee that no sorcerer will ever again be able to weave anything in secret, anywhere. Your eye would never again be a shame, Naya. It would be the guardian of the world. I am offering you exactly what you left to seek on the roads: a place where what you are is good for something."

And Naya felt the ground open beneath her, because there was still no smoke, and because a part of her, a part she hated with all her strength, wanted to say yes.

That is when she truly understood, for the first time in her life, something her gift had never taught her: lies are not the only danger in the world. One can want the good, want it sincerely, with all one's soul, and build a prison. The grey smoke does not protect you from that. Nothing protects you from that, except knowing how to say no to an honest man.

She looked at the young man with the open face, and answered softly:

"In your perfect world, Sètondji, who will have the right to tell you no?"

For the first time since their arrival, Sètondji's smile faltered.

"When everything is stable..."

"Who?" Naya repeated. "The farmer of Sèmè could not say no. The fairies of the lake could not say no. Georgia sleeps in a beautiful dream she did not choose. You may be the best man in the kingdom, and that is precisely the problem: your world only works if you stay that way forever. No one stays that way forever. Kadel too began with a true injustice."

The silence lasted a long time. Then Sètondji nodded slowly, and there was in his eyes something that looked like genuine regret.

"You would have been my finest ally." He rose. "You are free to go, all of you. I keep only what is useful, and angry prisoners are of no use to me. Go back to Black-Fish, Majesty. Think over my offer. It will remain open until the end."

"And if we come back with an army?" asked Jordan, rising in his turn.

Sètondji looked at him with perfect tranquillity, and that is when Naya finally saw something come out of his mouth. Not grey smoke.

Certainty, pure and cold as ivory.

"Then you will lose, Majesty. And the saddest part is that I will still be obliged to keep a place for you in the world after."

They left that same day, escorted to the gates with courtesy. And all through the journey home, Naya could not chase away one thought: of all the dangers she had seen since Kpassè, the most terrifying was a man who never lied.
