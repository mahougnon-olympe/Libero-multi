# Chapter 25: The Trap in the Ruins

The fortress appeared on the morning of the third day, and it was more terrible than anything Naya had imagined.

On the black ruins of Kadel's old lair, someone had built new: high, straight walls, clean towers, solid roofs. This was not the den of a cursed sorcerer. It was a city being born, ordered, almost beautiful, and that was what froze the blood. On the roads leading to it, lines of carts advanced, pulled by empty-eyed marked, and to Naya's eye, the whole sky above the fortress was a monstrous knot of green threads converging from every horizon.

They did not have to look for a way in.

A thousand paces from the walls, the great gate opened by itself, and a single man came out, without weapon or escort, walking calmly to meet them. He was young, barely more than twenty-five, simply dressed, with an open, intelligent face you would have liked to have for a neighbour.

"King Jordan," he said, bowing with a respect that held no mockery. "And you, you must be the seer my sentinels have been talking about since Danko. I am Sètondji. I expected you a little earlier, to be honest. Come in, please. You have come a long way to see me, and I have nothing to hide."

Naya had expected anything but that. She looked for the grey smoke around his mouth.

There was none.

They went in: twelve soldiers on edge, a king with a face of stone, and a sixteen-year-old girl looking everywhere. And Sètondji gave them a tour. Truly. He showed them the full granaries, the clean dormitories where the marked slept between their hours of work, the workshops, the library where hundreds of grey notebooks lined the shelves.

"Your guests are slaves," said Jordan.

"My guests are fed, housed and cared for better than in their villages," Sètondji replied without a flicker. "They do not suffer, fear nothing, lack nothing. I know what you are thinking, Majesty, and you are right to think it: I have taken their will, and that is a crime. I know it. I own it. It is a smaller crime than the ones the world commits every day through negligence. When everything is in place, I will free them, and they will wake in a world without war, without famine and without mad sorcerers. Perhaps they will curse me. They will be alive to do it."

And still no grey smoke. Naya felt panic rising in her, a new panic that no lie had ever given her: this man believed every word he said.

Then Sètondji opened the doors of the great hall, and Naya forgot everything else.

At the centre, turning slowly on itself, floated a cocoon of light tall as three men, woven of thousands of green and gold threads, so dense, so bright, that even the soldiers without any gift stepped back, shielding their eyes. All the threads of the kingdom ended there. The cable from the fairy lake plunged into its summit like a river into the sea.

And inside, floating at the heart of the light, eyes closed, long hair rippling gently like waterweed, slept a woman Naya recognised without ever having seen her, because all the tales of her childhood slept with her.

"Georgia," Jordan breathed.

He took a step. Twenty marked came out of the shadow of the pillars, soundlessly, and simply placed themselves between the king and the cocoon. Not threatening. Just there. Farmers, mothers, a teenager. A wall of innocent people.

"She does not suffer," said Sètondji with a sincere gentleness that was worse than any cruelty. "She dreams. I keep her in a beautiful dream, I designed it myself; in it she protects people and they love her. Her power is the greatest the earth has ever carried, Majesty. Thanks to her, all of this holds. Thanks to her, the world I am building will be possible. In the end, it is she, once again, who will have saved it."

He turned to them, and his open face broke into an almost shy smile.

"And now that you have seen everything, I would like to make you an offer."
