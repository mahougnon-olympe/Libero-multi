# Chapter 9: The Words That Had to Be Said

They walked all morning in silence. The landscape changed little by little: the grass grew greener, the air softer, and small white flowers that neither of them had ever seen now lined the path. The lake was near. You could feel it.

At noon they stopped near a spring to eat Sena's provisions. It was there that Jordan finally broke the silence.

"Georgia. I need to talk to you. Before the lake."

She looked up from her meal. The prince was sitting across from her, upright, his hands resting on his knees, like a defendant before his judge.

"Ever since the village of Toko, I can't stop thinking about it. If the lake heals me tomorrow, everyone will say: it wasn't his fault, it was the spell. My aunt will say it. The guards will say it. Maybe even you will say it. And it would be a lie."

He drew a long breath.

"Kadel darkened my heart, that's true. But what he found there, I was the one who put it there. The pride, the selfishness, the habit of taking whatever I wanted without ever asking... all of that was there long before him. The spell only opened a door I had never bothered to close."

He looked her straight in the eyes, and his voice began to tremble.

"What I did to you at the castle is unforgivable. I'm not asking you to forgive me. I never will, because you owe me nothing, and because some things cannot be erased with an apology. I just wanted you to hear it from my own mouth, with no spell to serve as my excuse: I hurt you, Georgia. Me. And I will carry that my whole life."

A long silence settled in. All that could be heard was the murmur of the spring.

"Do you know why I saved you, in the Wassa forest?" Georgia said at last. "That day, I told myself: no one is born evil. I wanted to believe it. But the truth is, I wasn't sure. I saved you wondering whether I wasn't making the biggest mistake of my life."

She stood and stepped toward the spring.

"I do not forgive you, Jordan. Not today. Perhaps not for a long time. What you did to me, I carry it too, and I'm the one who will decide what to do with it, and when. But what you just said... it's the first time I've seen you face what you did without hiding behind anything. And that counts."

She turned back toward him.

"So here's what I propose. We finish this quest. You drink the water of the lake. And afterward, you spend the rest of your life becoming the man you just showed me for five minutes. Not for me. Not to be forgiven. Because it is right. Deal?"

Jordan nodded slowly, unable to speak.

"Good," said Georgia, picking up her bag. "Then on your feet, Prince. We have a lake to find."

They took to the road again, side by side. And although neither of them said it aloud, something had changed between them. It was not friendship, not yet. It was rarer than that: it was respect.

At the top of the last hill, they stopped short.

Down below, nestled in a setting of white flowers, the fairy lake shimmered with a thousand silver reflections, as if someone had poured all the stars of the sky into a crystal bowl. Little lights danced above the water: the fairies.

It was the most beautiful place they had ever seen.

And standing on the shore, waiting for them, was Kadel.
